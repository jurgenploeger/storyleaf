# Tales of Dorlune: notes for Claude sessions

A Fairyland Online (2007) tribute RPG for iPhone: SwiftUI + SpriteKit, project generated by XcodeGen from
`project.yml`. Stay faithful to how Fairyland Online played and looked; check before inventing new systems.
Players know it as **Tales of Dorlune** (since 0.8.11, 2026-10-25: the App Store name and the logo; the Home
Screen shows **Tales of Dorlune** too (since 0.9.1; Dorlune before), project.yml `CFBundleDisplayName`, and the land itself, in every in-game line, is Dorlune;
it was Talewild from 0.8.1 to 0.8.10). The code and project are **TalesOfDorlune** (`TalesOfDorlune/`,
`TalesOfDorluneTests/`, the TalesOfDorlune target and scheme, types like `TalesOfDorluneSky`, the
`TALES_OF_DORLUNE_DEBUG` flags) and the repo tales-of-dorlune, since 2026-10-26; they were Storyleaf before, and
Fairyland before 2026-10-22. Never write Storyleaf anywhere new. Some things keep an old name on purpose, as
players' games are tied to them: the bundle ID (`com.jurgenploeger.fairyland`, which App Store Connect, TestFlight
and every player's saves are tied to), the save files' names (`fairyland-save.json`), and content ids saved in a
game (the weaver's `storyleaf_cape`, shown as Dorlune Cape). Don't bring back Fairyland Online's own names (towns,
people, bosses, quests) in player-facing text: `docs/fairyland-online.md` → Names.

## Layout
- `docs/fairyland-online.md`: how the original game (and Tales of Mysteria, its 2026 relaunch) worked, with
  sources, and where we differ. Read it before inventing a system; add facts you verify, with their source.
- `docs/continuous-world.md`: the plan for one continuous world (no borders, chunks streamed round you), in steps.
- `docs/sprite-acceptance.md`: what generated and fitted character art must pass (every body, direction and
  armour), how to check it, and what the Retro Diffusion wearables taught us. Read it before making or fitting any.
- `docs/pixel-style.md`: the game's pixel-art look, measured, for sprites drawn in code instead of by Retro
  Diffusion: the rules, the acceptance criteria (`tools/pixel_style.py check`, bands calibrated on every RD
  sprite the game ships, `tools/pixel_style.json`), the review by eye, and `tools/pixel_kit.py`, a renderer
  for the look (shapes and materials in, lit, textured, hue-shifted pixel art out). Read it before drawing
  a sprite in code; recalibrate after RD art is added.
- `docs/launch-audit.md`: the pre-launch audit (2026-10-06): App Store checklist, open bugs and balance issues
  ranked, and gamification ideas. Tick items off or remove them as they're done.
- Brand guidelines: the Figma file `521ECWenGbx2IP3rLgtIWo`, pages Cover to 09 Voice & Tone (logo, colour, type, icons,
  assets, game UI, App Store, voice). Its colours are variables (the Dorlune collection, with their Swift names; the text styles are Dorlune/…, the titles in Luckiest Guy); the logo is the component "Dorlune Logo" on the Workspace page, Luckiest Guy outlined to vectors;
  `Brand` in `TalesOfDorlune/UI/BrandSky.swift` holds the same values, so change both together.
- `content/world.json` + `tools/world_layout.py`: the lands as one continent, one landmass with one coast, each land a
  shape of its own (RuneScape-like regions, cold north, desert south). Edit world.json's `design` (each land's middle
  and lobes round it; a town's walled square, maps.json `town.size`, sits in its own countryside) and `rivers` (each
  one's course from its spring to the sea, or to a `lake` of its own) and rerun the tool (numpy + scipy): it
  paints the shapes and rivers, writes each land's box (maps.json `width`/`height`), middle, road crossings with
  their turns (a road into a cave ends inland, at the hill its mouth is in: `cave_gates`, `WorldMap.raiseGate`; a
  road by ship, maps.json exit `ship`, at a harbor on the coast, its pier out over the sea walked on though no land
  owns it: `harbors`, `World.pier`, `LandStage.placeHarbors`),
  and shape (`regions`, rivers in it), and `check_content.py` checks it (everything set down by hand on
  its land and clear of the rivers, roads meeting, nothing cut off by a river). Rerun it after changing exits or
  anything set down by hand; `--preview out.png` draws it. The game lays the rivers where they're painted, a bridge
  wherever a road crosses one (`WorldMap.layRivers`) along a row or column, so the tool runs each river straight along the other
  where a road crosses it (`SQUARE_REACH`; aslant, its bridge crossed it askew); the land slopes down to the sea and into the lakes
  (`WorldMap.shore`, drawn sunk by `HillArt`, darker the lower it lies; the sea paler in the shallows, `ContinentScene.shallows`). `TalesOfDorlune/World/Region.swift` is a land's shape; `Continent.swift` reads
  them: neighbours where shapes touch, open and walled borders (a border opens along its length once a road you can
  take joins the two lands, so none skips a quest), and the sea all round, each cell drawn by the nearest land
  (`BorderArt` draws the fences, rocks and city walls along the cell edges). Boxes overlap: a land lays out and
  draws only its own cells (`WorldMap.owns`; the rest is `.sea` or `.away`). `Seam.swift` is the band where one
  land's ground and scenery give way to the next's in patches, measured from the border; its `Blend` says what
  any cell shows the same from every land, so a tile meets the one past the border (that land's) cleanly.
  The world map (`WorldAtlas`) shows every land's minimap where it lies (`WorldOverview`, cached on disk).
  The continent is one scene (`ContinentScene`, docs/continuous-world.md): `World` holds which land owns each world
  cell and where you can walk; each land near you is laid out, planned (`LandPlanner`) and drawn a 32-cell chunk at a
  time round you, its ground by `GroundTiles`, its hills and shore by `HillArt.painter`, what it sets down and its
  people by `LandStage`, in its own points at its shift. Lands are laid out one at a time, nearest first (the one
  you're in before any), and drawn in their plain ground till then, walked on as their shape has it (anywhere but
  their rivers and lakes, `World.walkable`); the world map's own layouts wait meanwhile
  (`WorldOverview.isHeld`). You walk anywhere with no loading card; the land you're in
  (`here`) brings its music, air and encounters. Caves are scenes of their own (`WorldScene`), behind the card;
  `GameCoordinator.mapScene` is whichever you're in.
- `content/*.json`: all game data (maps, monsters, skills, items, quests, music, looks, crowd, announcements, the chat's
  filter (`moderation.json`, `ChatFilter`: what you type is starred out where it matches), titles,
  rewards: the daily gift, bounties, Monster Book milestones, monster cards, the weaver's status capes and the
  festive seasons with their hats). Data-driven; most features are a JSON edit.
- `art/assets.json` + `art/sprites/`: every sprite. `derive` makes free palette swaps of existing art. Recolour a whole
  colour family with one `to` + `spread` rule (`RecolorRule` in `TalesOfDorlune/Art/Recolor.swift`) so it keeps its hue-shifted
  shading; a bare `shift` lands on a different colour for every base, and a window that cuts a ramp splits it in two.
  Never swap a hero's whole sheet for a townsperson: hair shares its shades with skin and boots, so the deep reds and
  pale tips stay ginger. Derive them with `layers` instead (body, locks, hair from `tools/hero_layers.py`, the hair's
  rules widened to appearance.json `hairLayer`), as every quest giver is; `check_content.py` insists.
- Making any sprite: the skill `.claude/skills/sprite-creation` (which of the four routes, a palette swap, layers,
  code or Retro Diffusion, the steps for each, and the gate every new sprite passes: `scripts/sprite_check.py check
  <ids> --new` and `review` beside its neighbours; Retro Diffusion's styles, prices and prompts in its `references/`).
- `art/eyes.json`: where every sprite's eyes are, so it blinks (`EyeBlink`, `TalesOfDorlune/Art/IdlePoses.swift`):
  a single picture's (a lash drawn across each shut eye), a walk sheet's in each direction's standing pose (shut
  under its own lid); a body layer's stand for its head layer, a copy shuts its base's. The eyes are shut on the
  PNG or layer before it's recoloured and dressed (`ArtLibrary.idlePoses`), so hair and masks drawn over them
  still are. Never a wink: both eyes shut or neither (`EyeBlink.shutsEvenly`), and no blink where a helmet's or
  an armour sheet's own face shows round the eyes (`ArtLibrary.cleanFaces`). Standing still, a walk sheet also breathes in whole
  pixels (`EyeBlink.exhaled`: everything above the waist a pixel lower). A new monster or body needs its eyes
  here, or a reason in `skipped`; `check_content.py` checks each box and fill pixel. How to box them, the
  acceptance criteria and what went wrong before: the skill `.claude/skills/sprite-idle-animation` (its
  `scripts/eyes.py dump | zoom | fills | check` previews and checks every body, hairstyle, helmet and armour).
- `TalesOfDorlune/`: Swift code (World = maps and walking, Battle = combat, UI = SwiftUI HUD and menus).
- Item icons are in the clean look (2026-10-26, docs/pixel-style.md *Item icons*: RD's Potion, the user's pick;
  its *Acceptance criteria* checklist is what every new or changed icon must meet):
  64 pixels, lit from the top left on smooth ramps, no grain, a one-pixel outline in a deep shade of what it
  rounds; `tools/pixel_style.py check --kind item`. `tools/clean_icon_art.py` draws a few at 64 (the Seal Stones,
  the lucky clover; `item_art.py` skips them), `tools/coin_art.py` the coins.
- `tools/item_art.py`: designs every other item at 32×32 in code (free), finished in the clean look at 64 by
  `pixel_kit.restyle(clean=True)` (a palette swap of one, `derive`, is swapped on the design and finished too);
  rerun it after editing a drawing. Anything meant to be round (a ring, a pearl, an egg) is drawn with
  `exact=True` so it's cut exactly round at 64. Boots, gloves, capes and set hats share helpers (`boot`,
  `glove`, `cape`, `hat_piece`): fix the family there. A weapon also
  gets `held_<id>_side.png` and `_front.png` (`held_pair`), its grip in the middle of a 64-pixel canvas, one of its
  pixels to one of the hero's (`GearArt.posed`): its drawing about 24 pixels across walking left or right; facing
  you or away held up nearly upright in the hand (`FRONT_TURN`). Always in the same hand: in front of the body where
  it's nearer you (facing you, walking right), behind it where it's farther (held sideways from the front it looked
  wrong, off to the side it looked stood behind); a whip's lash hanging down to the feet (`hanging_whip`; held up like a sword it
  read as a stick). Shrunk and turned offline, each pixel the colour covering most of it: in the game it was ragged.
- Dark Mode: the game follows the phone's look, or Settings → Appearance (`GameSettings.appearanceKey`, set on the
  window by `RootView.applyAppearance`). `Color.adaptive(light:dark:)` (`TalesOfDorlune/UI/Style.swift`) gives the
  HUD's light surfaces (frames, plates, buttons, portraits) a dimmer twin and the glass a deeper one;
  lettering keeps its colours. The title and loading sky turns to night (`Brand.night*`, `TalesOfDorluneSky`), and
  text straight on it uses `Brand.skyText`. Debug flag `dark` (or `light`) forces a look for screenshots.
- `tools/app_icon_art.py`: the app icon, light, dark and tinted (iOS 18+), from one picked RD drawing
  (`SOURCE`: the game's own hero with a golden sword before the apple and blossom trees, built from the
  sprites and redrawn by RD), cropped in close on him, hair to knees over a band of meadow (`CROP`), its flat sky graded from deep
  blue overhead to a pale haze in dithered bands (`day_sky`), and scaled by a whole number; dark is the scene at dusk under the night `TalesOfDorluneSky` draws, lit by its gold, fireflies, sparkles
  and stars (`light_up`), in hard steps; tinted (iOS's Tinted and Clear looks) the hero bright, cut on his
  outline, on a dim backdrop (`tint`), as the system's own icons are a light figure on a dark field. `--preview` shows Home Screen sizes.
- `tools/season_icon_art.py`: the festive seasons' app icons (AppIcon-Halloween, -Christmas, -Easter), from the
  app icon's drawing cropped a little looser (`BOX`) so a hat fits: the hero as drawn (`HERO`), RD's scenery
  recoloured class by class, each colour onto the same rank of a hue-shifted seasonal ramp (its clusters kept),
  snow on the leaves' lit clusters, and hats and props drawn with `pixel_kit`, a hat's band on the head's widest row;
  each light, dark (its night, lit by the season's lights, `at_night`) and tinted, as the app icon is.
  The game switches to Halloween's all October, Christmas's all December and Easter's while its season runs (`SeasonalIcon.icon`; rewards.json `seasons` for Easter; never in tests or debug launches).
- `tools/logo_art.py`: the logo (`Logo.png`), styled on Neopets' "Tales of Dacardia" logo, which the user pointed to:
  TALES OF over DORLUNE in chunky Luckiest Guy capitals (`tools/fonts/`, Apache 2.0), set tight, each letter tipped
  and lifted a little so the word bounces, each in a thin outline of its own; all gold, each line in one smooth
  gradient with a light rim along the tops, in one thick dark outline dropping into a block shadow. One colour,
  no bands, no emblem, tagline or rays. The sky's light sits behind DORLUNE (`Brand.raysInLogo`).
- `tools/store_slides.py`: App Store slides from the landscape `store_land_*` screenshot scenes: captions in the
  logo's style, the title in its face and gold, in capitals, in its thick dark outline and block shadow, a tag on a dark plate in Open Sans (`tools/store_slides.json`, fonts in `tools/fonts/`), cropped clear of the Dynamic Island the
  simulator draws in, exported at 2622×1206 and 2868×1320 with no alpha.
- `tools/social_art.py`: the social media assets in `marketing/social/` (profile pictures from the icon's
  drawing round the hero's face; logo files; covers and banners for X, Bluesky, Facebook, YouTube, Discord,
  Instagram and the website's link preview, from the HUD-free `store_land_*` scenes with the logo in each
  platform's safe area, `SPECS`). `--shots DIR` takes the scenes fetched from CI's `screenshots` branch.
- `tools/weapon_art.py`: weapons from Retro Diffusion: a picked 64-pixel drawing (`PICKED`, from `art-variants/`)
  made the icon at its own size, its outline a deep shade of what it rounds (`icon`; `pixel_style.py --kind
  rd_item`), and shrunk to the held sprite at the drawn weapons' size; `item_art.py` leaves those
  alone. `tools/gear_preview.py --weapons out.png <ids>` shows weapons in hand on every body, direction and armour.
- `tools/skill_art.py`: every skill's icon (`art/sprites/skill_<id>.png`), drawn as light, not pixel art: the
  glows, flares, sparks and trails of its battle animation in its colours (`LIGHTS`, `ElementLight`'s), on a dark
  backdrop of its tile's colour, at 128 pixels. The game shows art over 64 pixels smoothed (`IconTile`,
  `BattleScene.skillTile`). A new skill needs a `draw_<id>`; `--sheet out.png` previews them on their tiles.
- `tools/armour_art.py`: the same for every armour's icon, from one picked RD drawing per shape (`PICKED`, `rda_<shape>`),
  coloured as it's worn: the sheets' `MATERIAL` and the item's `tint` (a hide cloak its hood's), so icon and hero match;
  at 64 pixels as a weapon's is (`weapon_art.icon`).
- `tools/dye_art.py`: dyes (`TalesOfDorlune/Content/Dyes.swift`) mirrored: `cut` cuts each armour sheet's and hat's
  trim into a layer of its own (`<sheet>_trim.png`, its `TRIMS` windows, never a hand, face or a dwarf's gold hair)
  and measures every material for `DyeDef.mains`/`trims`, and lists the one-colour weapons, `oneColour` (paste them in); `check` reads that table back and holds
  every dye on every kind, main and trim, gloves, boots, icons and held weapons, to reading as its colour;
  `--sheet out.png` shows them all. Rerun `cut` and `check` after changing a sheet or a dye rule.
- `tools/prop_art.py`: the same for the few props a generated sprite got wrong (`candy_cane`, whose hook came out
  as a two-sided arch) or none was made for (the harbors' `ship`, built in 3D and projected as the game's view is,
  then lit and finished as the buildings are and held to their bands, `form_relief` too;
  `cave_ladder`, the way down to a cave's deeper level, and `cave_rope`, the way back up, `depth` and NPC role
  `stairs` in maps.json; `lost_pile`, what you lost when you fell, lying where you fell (`LostPile`); `waystone`, NPC role `waystone`, a standing stone out in a wild land that a quest
  wakes, then a stop for travel like the towns: `GameSession.travelDestinations`).
  Rerun it after editing a drawing; `--sheet out.png` previews them.
- `tools/scenery_art.py`: trees and bushes drawn in code with `pixel_kit` (docs/pixel-style.md), one species
  each where every tree had been a recolour of RD's `tree` and every bush of its `berry_bush`: a cherry, a
  gnarled dark tree, an autumn oak, a maple, a birch, a frosted bare tree, a willow, a jacaranda, candy trees,
  and ten bushes (a fir sapling, a tumbleweed, a bramble...), and the fir under snow for the snow lands. Each writes its own PNG, which wins over the
  asset's `derive`; its `big_` twin, derived from it, follows. `--sheet out.png` previews them.
- `tools/building_art.py`: town houses and shops drawn in code with `pixel_kit`, so the towns don't build only
  with RD's few buildings and their recolours: a thatched and a timber-framed cottage, a stone townhouse, a

  buildings that belong to a land: a pavilion tent, a bazaar stall and an onion-domed house for the deserts, a
  yurt for the steppe, a tree house, an acorn cottage, a bluebell house and a crystal cottage for Starfall
  Isle's Glimmerbough (maps.json `buildings`); and Briarmere's lakeside shrine to Liora (`lake_shrine`), the Warrior Guild's hall and the Wildfolk Lodge,
  a bell tower and a gem workshop; and a land's own landmarks, Emerald Road's Emerald Spire and Starfall Isle's
  Star Observatory and Moonwell. Not all one size: a grand building (`grand(160)`, the two guild halls,

  gives it the room (building `size`, its footprint in cells). Walls

  foot to fill it as RD's do (`fit`). Registered in art/assets.json with no `request`, placed by maps.json's

- `tools/crystal_art.py`: the Glimmerdeep's crystal scenery from the caves' own rock (its walls, floor, spires and
  geodes): each pixel's brightness laid on a ramp of crystal colours, as a `derive` can't tint the grey tiles.
  Rerun it after editing a ramp; `--sheet out.png` previews them.
- `tools/ui_icon_art.py`: the UI's button icons (`art/sprites/ui_<GameIcon>.png`), drawn as light in the skill
  icons' style with `skill_art.Icon` (glossy shapes, flares, a soft shadow; `UIIcon` drops the shapes' coloured
  glow), at 128 pixels on no backdrop, every glow and shadow kept to a thin rim round the shapes (`HALO`), so
  nothing smudges or gets cut off on a light button: `IconImage` shows them smoothed from 18 pt up (battle buttons, the HUD's buttons, the

  game's button colours.
- `tools/water_art.py`: the same for the water: `sea.png` (16 × 16 cells that tile as a whole; the continent gives each
  world cell its own, `ContinentScene.seaGroups`) and `tile_water.png` (a land's lakes and rivers, calm so its versions
  don't make a grid), ripples level on screen. Round a land's own water (`tile_water`, not ice or chocolate)
  `ArtLibrary.organicTile` adds depth and a bank: paler shallows by the shore, deeper water out from it (two

- `tools/fx_art.py`: the same for the few battle marks that aren't light (`art/sprites/fx_*.png`: poison's bubble

  stats). `--sheet out.png` writes a contact sheet. Spells are light, not sprites:
  `TalesOfDorlune/Battle/ElementEffects.swift` layers soft glows in each element's colours (`ElementLight`).
- `tools/coin_art.py`: the coins in the Bag (`item_coins_1` to `_4`, a bigger pile the more you carry,

- `tools/lair_art.py`: the pieces of the bosses' lairs no sprite had (a standing stone, a brazier, a gold hoard, a

  `pixel_kit` and scored as scenery. A boss's lair is maps.json `lair` on the boss: pieces round it, its way in to

  trail's end, on a summit, or in a cave; `check_content.py` keeps the way in clear.
- `tools/city_wall_art.py`: a city's stone wall and round towers (`city_wall`, `city_tower`, placed by
  `TownFence.place` for town `walls: "stone"`), drawn as the town gate's rubble is, graded with the map's palette.
  The wall is one cell's stretch that tiles side to side. `--sheet out.png` previews them.
- `tools/gate_art.py`: each town's own gate over its roads (maps.json town `gate`: Meadowbrook's thatched
  lychgate, Prismhaven's crystal keystone, Ingothold's gold and red cloth, Larkspur's bluebird); quests'
  gates across borders keep RD's `town_gate`. Each is laid out as `town_gate` is (`TownFence.widened` spans a

- `tools/critter_art.py`: the critters (`ambience.critters`), the birds crossing the sky and the butterflies

- `tools/hero_layers.py`: splits each race's walk sheet into paper-doll layers (bald `body_<race>`,
  `locks_<race>` for a beard, `hair_<style>_<race>` for every hairstyle in that sheet's own hair colour, a
  gender sheet's own hair as a style of its own (appearance.json `styles` with `sheet`), its outer edge a deep
  shade of its own colour (`hair_edge`: RD drew the top bright, and dyed purple it read as no outline), `hood_`/`helmet_<race>`

  dyes every shade of the locks and hair (appearance.json `hairLayer`), never the body. Armour with a walk sheet
  of its own goes over `nape_<style>_<body>` (the hair from a fifth of the way down the head), so long hair
  shows below the helmet, and from behind `napeback_<style>_<body>` (its back view on the head) goes over the
  bare head, so hair, not scalp, shows under a helmet; one that leaves the head bare (items.json `bareHead`, wear_art.py `BARE_HEADED`: every
  armour sheet, as the hat slot is what goes on the head) is cut off at the neck, the hero's `head_<body>`, locks and hair over it, and what's cut off is its hat
  (`hat_<kind>_<race>`, a hat item of its own: items.json `headgear`, the armour's `headpiece`). `TOWNSFOLK` sheets no hero wears are split too (`dwarf_npc`: the old bearded dwarf,
  his own mop as `hair_dwarf_npc`, a mouth drawn into his beard by `tools/character_art.py mouth`). The bald head is painted only inside the sprite as drawn (`_scalp`), so a
  body wearing its own hair matches its sheet: a skull painted past a side-view face stuck out like a brow ridge.

  `tools/gear_preview.py`, `--wear out.png <ids>` shows items on every body under every armour): a hat widened to the

- `tools/rd.py`: Retro Diffusion client. `rd.py edit` inpaints a mask of an existing sheet (pilot 2026-10-19: inpainting
  of walk sheets is a dead end: faces, about 10 px wide, come out muddy, and armour or a robe painted onto a hero comes
  out too bulky, with side and back views that don't match the pose), its results only in
  `art-variants/<name>/` (`rd.py fetch` gets any that didn't download). `tools/character_art.py` makes the masks (face,
  body, helmet) from `hero_layers.py`'s heads, into `art-variants/_masks/`, outside `art/` so they never ship. `tools/icons.py`: vendors Iconaut icons (MIT), plus our own drawings in
  `art/icons/` where Iconaut has no shape that works (fire's three-tongued flame).
  `tools/check_content.py`: validates the game data.
- `tools/deslab.py`: cuts the raised slab of ground some generated buildings came with (`inn`, `magic_tower`) out
  of their sprites, so they stand on the map's ground; rerun it after regenerating one of them.
- `theme.palette` in `content/maps.json` colour-grades each map's ground, scenery and buildings at load time.
  `tools/palette_preview.py` (needs pillow + numpy) renders every map with and without it, to tune without a Mac.
- `content/i18n/`: translations. `languages.json` lists the languages (the picker's order), `<code>.json` maps each
  English string to its translation, `fields.json` names the content keys that hold text. Swift text goes through
  `L("English {placeholder}", ["placeholder": value])` (`TalesOfDorlune/Model/Localization.swift`); content text is
  translated as it loads (`Content.reload` after a switch). `tools/i18n.py status | missing <code> | merge | prune`.
- `content/music.json` defines instruments and songs for `TalesOfDorlune/Audio/SongSynth.swift`. `tools/music_preview.py`
  (needs numpy + scipy) renders songs to WAV with the same algorithm; keep the two in step.
- `sound/*.wav`: sound effects, synthesized by `tools/make_sounds.py` (needs numpy + scipy; 44.1 kHz stereo,
  byte-identical on every rerun). Edit a recipe and rerun (`--report` prints each file's length and loudness);
  `SoundEffects.Sound` in `TalesOfDorlune/Audio/SoundEffects.swift` lists them. Settings live in `GameSettings` (UserDefaults).

## Rules
- **Cloud sessions (Linux) can't build or run the app.** After editing `content/` or `art/assets.json`, run
  `python3 tools/check_content.py`. Keep Swift edits small and careful: the Screenshots workflow compiles
  them on a `claude/**` branch, and `.github/workflows/testflight.yml` after a push to `main` (Xcode Cloud
  isn't used: its GitHub app was uninstalled on 2026-10-23).
- **Never redraw Retro Diffusion's art in code** (the user, 2026-10-24: "that's actually really good"). Every
  asset with a `request` in art/assets.json is RD's and stays as it is, and so does what's `derive`d from it.
  Draw in code only what RD never made, or what it got wrong and the user asked to have fixed.
- **Retro Diffusion costs money.** Always run `python3 tools/rd.py generate <ids> --dry-run` (free) and get
  the user's OK before generating. Paid runs are logged in `art/spend-log.jsonl`. Never commit `.env` or the
  API key (`RETRO_DIFFUSION_API_KEY`).
- **Every skill looks different** (a playtest rule): each skill used in battle has its own `animation` in
  `content/skills.json`, drawn by `TalesOfDorlune/Battle/SkillStyles.swift` (or `ElementEffects.swift` for the elemental
  spells) and dispatched in `BattleScene.castSkill`. `check_content.py` rejects a shared or unknown one, so a new skill
  needs a new effect, in light and its own colours.
- **Every player-visible string is translated:** wrap new Swift text in `L("…")` (a single-line literal; values
  as `{placeholders}`, never `\(` inside the key), then fill in all ten languages with `tools/i18n.py missing <code>`
  and `merge`. Text in content fields listed in `content/i18n/fields.json` needs the same. Untranslated text shows in
  English; `check_content.py` rejects lost placeholders.
- **Versions:** `MARKETING_VERSION` in `project.yml` and the top entry of `content/changelog.json` move together
  (the checker enforces it). What's new sums up big updates, not every patch: one entry per update with at most
  five short lines (the checker enforces it). A small user-visible change moves the top entry's `version` and
  `date` along and folds into its lines; start a new entry only for a big update. 1.0.0 = App Store launch.
- **Screenshots and tests:** pushes to `claude/**` branches run `.github/workflows/screenshots.yml` (macOS
  runner): it builds for the simulator, runs the unit tests (a job of its own, its own check) and
  shoots the scenes in `tools/screenshots.txt` (TALES_OF_DORLUNE_DEBUG flags); a scene that crashes the app prints
  its crash report in the log. Results
  land on the `screenshots` branch under `<branch>/`: `git fetch origin screenshots` and read the PNGs.
  While the repo is public that's free; once it's private, macOS minutes count ten times against 2,000 a
  month, so pushes run nothing: run it by hand (`workflow_dispatch`) with `run` (tests, screenshots or
  both) and the `scenes` you need, as few as will do.
- On a Mac: `make open` or `make sim`; tests with `xcodebuild test` (see README). Tests and debug launches use
  separate save files, so they never touch the player's save.
